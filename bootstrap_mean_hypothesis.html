Hypothesis testing

A hypothesis test is a statistical technique for evaluating two competing possibilities, referred to as the "null" hypothesis and the "alternative" hypothesis. The test estimates how often means at least as extreme as the sample would occur in the direction of the alternative hypothesis, assuming that the null hypothesis is true.

If means at least as extreme as the observed sample mean are very unlikely to occur, then maybe the null hypothesis assumption is false.

The null hypothesis

This app "centers" the sample data by subtracting the difference between the null mean you entered above and the sample mean from the data values. After subtracting, the modified sample data have a mean equal to the null mean value you entered.

The null hypothesis in this app assumes that the centered sample data are reasonably representative of the population (sometimes, this may not be the case!). One null distribution bootstrap mean is then calculated by resampling (sampling with replacement) from the centered sample data. This process is repeated 10000 times to generate the histogram shown above.

Sample size

The actual number of total observations in your observed sample.

Sample mean

The mean of your sample data (without centering).

Alternative

p-value

The total count of bars in the histogram above is 10000. The p-value is simply the count in the dark-blue shaded bars divided by the total count. It estimates how likely means at least as extreme as what was observed in the sample would occur under the assumption that the null hypothesis is true. If the p-value is tiny, then maybe the null hypothesis is false!
