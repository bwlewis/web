Parametric Bootstrap Hypothesis Test for a Single Proportion

This app tests a proportion: the sum of ``yes'' observations divided by the total number of observations. (Any binary observations work, like "yes" or "no", 1 or 0, A or B, etc.) The app resamples the null distribution using the specified null proportion. Enter the count of observed "yes" values in your sample and select an alternative hypothesis to calculate the p-value.

Hypothesis testing

A hypothesis test is a statistical technique for evaluating two competing possibilities, referred to as the "null" hypothesis and the "alternative" hypothesis. A hypothesis test estimates how often the alternative hypothesis is likely to occur assuming that the null hypothesis is true.

If proportions at least as extreme as the observed proportion are very unlikely to occur, then maybe the null hypothesis assumption is false.

The null hypothesis

The app simulates one null distribution sample by randomly generating observations, each with a chance of being "yes" (set above by the Null proportion and sample size) then recording the total count of "yes" divided by (the sample proportion). It repeats this 10000 times to generate the histogram shown.

Sample size

The actual number of total observations in your observed sample.

Observed proportion

The count of "yes" observations in your observed sample.

Alternative

p-value

The total count of bars in the histogram above is 10,000. The p-value is simply the count of the dark-blue shaded bars divided by the total count. It estimates how likely proportions at least as extreme as what was observed in the sample would occur under the assumption that the null hypothesis is true. If the p-value is tiny, then maybe the null hypothesis is false!

Exercises

Polls and Votes (right-tailed): In a poll of one stats class of 24 students, 14 prefer an oral presentation final exam, 10 prefer a non-oral written one. Is there evidence from this poll that all statistics students at Garrett College would vote for an oral exam?

  • What is the sample size?
  • What is the alternative hypothesis?
  • What is the null hypothesis?
  • Set up the problem using the app.
  • Pick a statistical discernability level (significance level). Using that level, can you conclude whether the vote would pass or fail?

Usability Test (right-tailed): A website team tests 50 randomly selected first-time users from its target audience. Each user attempts the same signup task once; 38 complete it without help. Let p be the proportion of all first-time users in the target audience who would complete the task without help. Does this sample provide statistically discernable evidence, at the 5% level, that p is greater than 0.70?

  • What is the sample size?
  • What is the alternative hypothesis?
  • What is the null hypothesis?
  • Set up the problem using the app. What p-value does it estimate, and what is your conclusion at the 5% level?

Coffee Roaster Calibration (two-tailed): A local coffee roaster calibrates its packaging machine so that 15% (0.15) of bags receive a promotional "golden ticket" sticker. To check if the machine has drifted out of calibration in either direction, a worker inspects a random sample of 80 bags and finds 6 with stickers. Let p be the true proportion of all bags receiving a sticker. Does this sample provide statistically discernable evidence, at the 5% level, that the machine is out of calibration (p ≠ 0.15)?

  • What is the sample size?
  • What is the alternative hypothesis?
  • What is the null hypothesis?
  • Set up the problem using the app. What p-value does it estimate, and what is your conclusion at the 5% level?
