Spurious correlation, p-hacking, and gardens of forking paths

Wikipedia says:
A spurious correlation is a mathematical relationship in which two or more events or variables are associated but not causally related, due to either coincidence or the presence of a certain third, unseen factor (referred to as a confounding factor or latent variable).

Tyler Vigen famously illustrates hilarious spurious correlations here: https://tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations. For instance, "butter consumption" is highly correlated to the number of doctors in Colorado!

But spurious correlation is not always funny. It can occur intentionally and accidentally:

  • P-hacking: Intentionally testing many combinations of variables, dropping data, or altering analyses until an artificially "statistically significant" result is found.
  • The Garden of Forking Paths: Unintentionally finding patterns by making analysis choices after seeing the data, unwittingly inflating the chance of finding a random false positive.
Sadly, both kinds regularly occur in the media and even the scientific literature.

Spurious correlation is likely to happen whenever you have lots of variables and few observations (it can occur other times too). As a general rule, whenever this situation occurs beware of possible spurious correlation.
